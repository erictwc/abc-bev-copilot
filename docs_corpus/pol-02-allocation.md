# Allocation policy

This policy says when a SKU goes on allocation, who decides, and how short stock is
shared out.

## When a SKU goes on allocation

A SKU goes on allocation when warehouse cover drops under two weeks, or when a co-packer
run for it is cut.

## Who decides

1. The Production Manager starts the allocation.
2. The Portfolio Manager takes it next.
3. Sales Operations has a chance to weigh in.
4. The Commercial VP is the final arbitrator.

## Priority between SKUs

1. A promoted SKU comes first when its promo is running or starts within 4 weeks.
2. Regular SKUs come next, ranked by popularity: rolling six-month depletions (see the
   metric library, MET-01).
3. Launch SKUs come last. They can always be pushed back.

## Splitting stock between distributors

Distributors are served in order of net profitability.

Each distributor keeps its strategic retailers in stock ahead of its other accounts. A
retailer is more strategic the more locations it has.

## Moving stock between warehouses

Stock can be moved between WH-EAST, WH-CENTRAL and WH-WEST to cover a shortage. Supply
Planning decides. There is no limit on transfers.

## Promoted SKUs at risk

Protect the promo. Running out during a promo is not the biggest problem: a promo
should sell out, which is the point of it, and selling out builds a fear of missing out.

## Telling distributors

Distributors are told when the allocation happens. They can appeal: appeals usually go
to Sales Operations and the Portfolio Manager.

## Coming off allocation

A SKU comes off allocation when there is ample supply across the supply chain: 30 days
of cover.
